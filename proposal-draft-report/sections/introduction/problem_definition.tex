\subsection{Problem Definition}

Modern communication systems increasingly rely on VoIP-based platforms for voice transmission, including online meetings, internet calls, and voice-based authentication services. At the same time, advancements in AI-driven speech synthesis and voice conversion technologies have enabled the generation of highly realistic synthetic speech that can closely mimic human identity and speech patterns. Despite significant progress in deepfake speech detection research, most existing systems are developed and evaluated under controlled, offline conditions using clean audio datasets. These systems typically fail to maintain reliable performance in real-world VoIP communication environments where audio signals are degraded due to compression codecs, packet loss, network noise, and streaming latency constraints.

This creates a critical gap between research-based detection models and practical deployment scenarios. There is currently no robust, low-latency system that can effectively detect synthetic speech in real-time while simultaneously handling the distortions introduced by VoIP communication channels.
