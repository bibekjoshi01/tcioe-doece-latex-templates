\subsection{Background}

The latest advancements made in Artificial Intelligence (AI), especially those in Text-to-Speech (TTS) and Voice Conversion (VC) technologies have make it possible to synthesize highly realistic synthetic speech with capabilities of mimicking human voice. With advancement in deep learning models and neural vocoders, it has become possible for modern-day machines to synthesize speech that sounds very much like authentic human speech. Although there are many useful applications of TTS technology in terms of assistive technologies, virtual assistants, and media production, they also poses significant threats in terms of security and privacy.

Google introduced a speech synthesis model names Tacotron in 2017 \cite{tacotron2017}, and subsequent research introduced prosody (intonation and stress)~\cite{prosody2018}, style control \cite{styletokens2018}, multi-speaker synthesis cite \cite{transfer2018}, among many more advances. Noting this growing increase in quality and, as such, misplaces trust, researchers also explore how synthesized speech can be detected.

Speech synthesis and voice impersonation are being used widely in various forms of deception, such as social engineering and fraud. Recent advances in neural speech synthesis have enabled realistic voice cloning attacks, where an attacker can impersonate a target individual’s voice using only a few seconds of reference audio. These attacks have been demonstrated to bypass human perception and even degrade performance of existing speaker verification systems \cite{firc2023deepfake}.

Deepfake speech detection systems work well when used in offline settings; however, the performance of these solutions degrades in real-life communications, which involves issues like VoIP compression, codec distortions, packet loss, noise, and streaming latency. With the continued growth of online communications, including internet calling, conferencing, and voice-base authentication systems, there is a growing need for robust and low-latency detection mechanisms which are capable of operating under degraded communication conditions in near real-time environments.
