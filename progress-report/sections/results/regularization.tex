\subsection{XGBoost Regularization Sweep}

An initial XGBoost configuration (deeper trees, 500 estimators, no additional L1/L2 penalty beyond scikit-learn defaults) was compared against a shallower, more heavily regularized configuration (reduced tree depth, increased \texttt{min\_child\_weight}, added L1/L2 regularization, fewer estimators). Table~\ref{tab:xgb_regularization} reports training and development EER for both.

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{8pt}
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{XGBoost Regularization Sweep (EER, \%)}
\label{tab:xgb_regularization}

\hline
\textbf{Configuration} & \textbf{Training EER} & \textbf{Development EER} & \textbf{Train--Dev Gap} \\
\hline
Initial (deep, unregularized) & 0.01 & 20.88 & 20.87 \\
Final (shallow, L1/L2-regularized, selected) & 0.10 & 6.66 & 6.56 \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The initial configuration showed near-zero training EER alongside substantially higher development EER, a signature of memorization rather than generalizable spoof-detection behavior. The regularized configuration reduced this gap by more than a factor of three. This comparison was conducted concurrently with the WavLM and duration configuration changes reported in Sections~6.1--6.2; the reduction in development EER shown here therefore reflects the combined effect of hyperparameter regularization together with the improved feature representation and duration configuration, rather than regularization in isolation.
