\subsection{Baseline Model Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison} reports the final comparison across all eight baseline models. Each model is trained on the full ASVspoof5 training partition (182,357 utterances) and evaluated exactly once on the held-out evaluation partition, consistent with this project's single-pass evaluation discipline; precision, recall, F1, and ROC-AUC are computed at the decision threshold calibrated on the development set. Classical ML entries use the selected WavLM-large, all-layer-averaged, fixed-3.0-second-duration configuration established in Sections~6.1--6.3. Development-set EER is now available for RawNet2 (5.87\%) but not yet for AASIST; supporting metrics (precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC) were not captured for either model in the current training run. Evaluation-set EER, the primary comparison metric, is available for all eight models.

Ranked by evaluation-set EER, the primary comparison metric, NeXt-TDNN (7.36\%) and SVM (7.69\%) are the strongest baselines, followed by GBM (9.55\%), Random Forest (12.85\%), XGBoost (13.20\%), and Spectro-AASIST (15.09\%); RawNet2 (36.04\%) and AASIST (29.12\%) trail well behind the rest. This is a notable result: AASIST and RawNet2 are widely reported to achieve sub-1\% EER on the ASVspoof 2019 LA benchmark, yet substantially underperform both the classical baselines and the lighter-weight NeXt-TDNN on ASVspoof5 in this project's current training run. Since both models use the same full-utterance training configuration described in Section~5.3, this gap is flagged as a priority to investigate before the final report rather than resolved into a definitive explanation here. RawNet2's newly captured development-set EER (5.87\%) is competitive with the classical baselines (comparable to XGBoost's 6.28\% and Random Forest's 6.46\%), which sharpens rather than resolves the question: under-training would be expected to produce a poor development-set score as well, so RawNet2's good development-set performance alongside its poor evaluation-set performance points instead toward a generalization gap between the attack types seen during training and development and the 16 wholly unseen attack algorithms introduced only in the evaluation partition (Section~5.1). Whether the same explanation extends to AASIST cannot yet be confirmed, since its development-set EER has not been captured.

Among the classical baselines, Random Forest offers the most balanced operating point (98.5\% precision, 92.2\% recall, F1 0.953), while XGBoost's near-perfect precision (0.998) comes at the cost of substantially lower recall (0.734), indicating that it rejects almost no genuine bonafide speech but also misses more spoofed utterances. Spectro-AASIST shows the opposite pattern among the deep models with supporting metrics available: low precision (0.597) with moderate recall (0.849), suggesting it over-flags bonafide speech as spoofed, consistent with its higher EER relative to the classical baselines.
